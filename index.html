Paths into skilled work

There is more than one way to begin

The right route depends on the occupation, local licensing rules, cost, learning style and available opportunities.

Earn-and-learn apprenticeship

A paid job with structured on-the-job learning and classroom instruction. Registered opportunities are offered by employers and program sponsors.

Technical school or community college

Build classroom and lab foundations before or alongside employment. Compare total cost, completion rates, employer connections and credentials.

High-school CTE or dual enrollment

Career and technical education can let students explore equipment, safety and career fields before graduation and may offer credit or credentials.

Employer or union training

Some employers and labor organizations hire beginners and provide structured training. Entry requirements and availability vary by area.

Military technical training

Military occupations can provide technical training and experience, but service commitments and civilian credential transfer should be researched carefully.

Start as a helper or trainee

Some careers allow entry-level work while skills grow under supervision. Ask how training, raises, credentials and advancement are documented.

Official places to continue your research

Search Registered Apprenticeships • Find local training • Research occupations and pay

Training requirements, licensing and wages vary by occupation and location. Verify information with official state agencies, employers and program sponsors before enrolling.
